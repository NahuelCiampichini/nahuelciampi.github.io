# RAM Shortage Report

The global RAM shortage is driving prices up and supply down across consumer, enterprise, and mobile markets.

---

## What Is the RAM Shortage and Why Is It Happening?

If you've tried to buy a stick of DDR5 lately — or ordered a laptop, server, or smartphone — you may have noticed prices climbing and stock running thin. This is the RAM shortage, and it's affecting the entire tech industry.

### What's Going On?

Global DRAM supply has tightened significantly since late 2025. The main causes are:

**AI infrastructure demand** — The explosion of large language model training has created enormous appetite for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and server DRAM. Cloud providers are buying in bulk, leaving less inventory for the consumer market.

**Fab consolidation** — The three major DRAM manufacturers (Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron) have been prioritizing enterprise-grade chips over consumer DDR5.

**Export restrictions** — Ongoing limits on semiconductor equipment exports have slowed capacity expansion at several Asian fabs, creating a supply ceiling that demand has now hit.

### Who's Affected?

- PC builders paying 30–60% more for DDR5 kits than 18 months ago
- Laptop OEMs stretching lead times or shipping base configs with minimal RAM
- Data centers competing directly with consumers for the same chips
- Smartphone makers absorbing cost increases or cutting specs

### What to Expect

Analysts expect the shortage to persist through at least mid-2027, when new fab capacity from Samsung's Texas plant and Micron's Idaho expansion is projected to come online.

---

## How the RAM Shortage Is Hitting PC Builders

For DIY PC enthusiasts, the RAM shortage has made one of the cheapest upgrade paths suddenly painful.

### Price Trends

A standard 32GB DDR5-6000 kit that cost around $80 in mid-2024 now retails for $130–$160. 64GB workstation kits have nearly doubled in some regions. DDR4 has also crept up as manufacturers wind down production in favor of DDR5.

### What You Should Do

**If you need RAM urgently:** Buy now. Prices are unlikely to drop in the near term, and waiting risks paying even more in six months.

**If you can wait:** Target Q3 or Q4 2027, when new fab supply should start easing prices.

**Consider used:** The used DDR4 market is healthy. Verify specs and test with MemTest86 before trusting any used kit.

### What to Avoid

- Overpriced RGB "gaming" kits — the underlying chips are identical to plain ones
- No-name brands with no warranty
- Buying more than you currently need

---

## AI Is Eating the World's RAM

The single biggest driver of the current RAM shortage isn't smartphones or gaming PCs — it's artificial intelligence.

### The Core Problem: HBM Is the Chokepoint

High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) — the stacked DRAM used in AI accelerators — is manufactured on the same equipment as conventional DRAM. When SK Hynix dedicates a fab line to HBM3E for Nvidia, those wafers can't produce consumer DDR5.

SK Hynix, which supplies the majority of HBM to Nvidia, has been running at full HBM capacity for over a year. Samsung is racing to qualify its own HBM3E. Micron is a distant third.

### The Trickle-Down Effect

1. HBM demand pulls fab capacity away from standard DRAM
2. Standard DRAM supply tightens
3. Enterprise RDIMM gets prioritized over consumer UDIMM
4. Consumer prices rise

It's not that AI companies are buying your DDR5 kit — it's that they're consuming the manufacturing capacity that would have produced it.

### Will It Get Better?

New HBM-specific fab lines are being built that won't compete with conventional DRAM. But those take 2–3 years to come online, and the AI infrastructure buildout shows no signs of slowing.
